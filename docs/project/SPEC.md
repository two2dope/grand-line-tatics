Build a single-file HTML pixel-art 3D naval-battle (Battleship-style) game called "Grand Line Tactics" (on-screen subtitle "Batalha na Grand Line"), in the same family as my other pixel sports artifacts (cricket, baseball, tennis, futsal, volleyball, gridiron): a low-resolution three.js scene of floating sea-plates on the Grand Line with a polished panel UI beside it. The whole game is in Brazilian Portuguese. It has Player vs CPU and online Player vs Player inside the published artifact. Use three.js r128 from cdnjs and Google Fonts only; vanilla JS, authored as separate source modules concatenated into one file. Pure-logic modules (rules, AI, protocol, RNG) must not touch the DOM or THREE, so they can be loaded headlessly in Node for tests.

LANGUAGE (PT-BR)
- <html lang="pt-BR">. Every visible string, aria-label, tooltip, log line and error lives in one dictionary T with plural helpers; no hard-coded text elsewhere. Correct accents and cedilla everywhere. Pick Google Fonts with Latin-Extended coverage (a pixel face for HUD/numbers, a pirate display face for titles) and verify that ç ã õ é ê í ó ú render.
- Numbers use Intl.NumberFormat('pt-BR'); bounties show the Berry sign (฿ 1.500.000.000). Tone is playful pirate talk: "Água!", "Acertou em cheio!", "Afundou o Going Merry!", "Sua vez, capitão!".
- Fan tribute: ships, crews and characters are the series', but all art is original procedural pixel art. Rip no assets and copy no official logos or artwork. Add a small footer disclaimer.

LOOK
- Render into a small target (pixel-size slider, default 2 px), nearest-filter upscale. Post pass: depth-texture outlines, every colour snapped to a fixed 90-colour palette in OKLab with Bayer dithering (palette tuned for sea, sky, wood, sail and fire hues). Orthographic isometric camera (azimuth, elevation, zoom) snapped to the pixel grid. Shadow map every frame; day, dusk, night and storm, with ship lanterns, torch buoys and a lighthouse beam at night.
- Two floating sea-plates, "Suas águas" and "Águas inimigas", each a grid of voxel water cells with an A–J / 1–10 coordinate rim. Cell colours are animated wave swells, with foam where ships sit. The rims are terraced, with banded soil and slate strata below, hanging vines and kelp, rock falls, floating chunks, and waterfalls pouring off the plate edges. A Reverse-Mountain-style canal flows UP a mountain to a sky island.
- Backdrop ring: the Red Line wall on the horizon, a Skypiea cloud island with the golden bell, Sabaody mangroves with rising bubbles, Marineford's split plaza with Marine warships in formation, Sea Kings surfacing, Laboon the whale, a Log Pose buoy between the plates, a Big Den Den Mushi as the giant video board (live shots, hits, ships sunk, turn timer, last shot call such as "B7 — Água!") and a smaller one on the other side. Add a dock crowd of instanced pirates and marines in both crews' colours; they cheer, wave flags and jump on hits and sinkings. Fireworks on victory.
- Doll's-house lift: split the backdrop ring into 8 sectors; the sectors facing the camera rise about 90 m with an eased 0.85 s motion, stop casting shadows and become hidden, so scenery never blocks the plates. Toggle with a "Erguer cenário próximo" checkbox. Disabled in the ceremony and in the panorama view.
- The ships are the series' ships, each modelled once from voxel parts and merged per material, with their canonical flags, sails and figureheads. The owner is shown by a hull-trim colour, a pennant and a plate border, never by changing the canon identity. Ships bob on the swell, with fluttering sails, wakes, recoiling cannons and tiny instanced deck crew. Fleet (length in cells):
  - Moby Dick, Whitebeard (5): whale-bow, crescent-moustache Jolly Roger.
  - Thousand Sunny, Straw Hats (4): lion figurehead, grass deck.
  - Oro Jackson, Roger Pirates (4): Adam-wood hull.
  - Red Force, Red-Haired Pirates (3): red-trim galleon.
  - Polar Tang, Heart Pirates (3): yellow submarine; it dives when sunk and leaves an oil slick.

  - Going Merry (2): sheep figurehead caravel.
- Shot FX: cannonball arcs with a trail and a muzzle flash, then a water splash ring on a miss. A hit shows fire, smoke and flying planks. A sinking ship tilts, goes under with an eased motion and bubbles, and its flag stays afloat. The sunk ship's outline is revealed on the enemy plate. Remaining ships take visible damage.
- Ceremony: the winner raises its Jolly Roger; a procedurally drawn pixel WANTED poster shows the captain, the crew's name and a bounty counting up. Bounty = f(accuracy, shots, speed, streaks, difficulty, rules).

GAME
- Six crews with captain, three named officers, Jolly Roger colours and a signature technique: Chapéus de Palha, Barba Branca, Cabelo Vermelho, Piratas do Coração, Barba Negra and Marinha.
- Boards ("formats", like Quick/Short/Full): Escaramuça 8x8 with 4 ships (4,3,3,2); Clássica 10x10 with 5 ships (5,4,3,3,2); Grand Line 12x12 with all 7 ships. Series length: melhor de 1, 3 or 5.
- Setup: drag-and-place ships in the 3D plate or the 2D grid; R, right-click or double-tap to rotate; validity ghost (green/red); auto-place, random, clear. Rule toggles: ships may touch, continue firing after a hit, salvo (shots per turn = ships alive), turn timer (off/20/30/60 s), fog (the last-shot marker fades after 2 turns). "Modo Tático" adds one crew technique per match. Techniques: Straw Hats' Bazooka (2x2 volley), Whitebeard's Tremor (5-cell cross), Red-Haired's Haki do Rei (yes/no sonar over a 3x3 area), Heart's Room (count of ship cells in one chosen row or column), Blackbeard's Vórtice (the next incoming shot is absorbed), Marines' Observação (reveals one cell of a surviving enemy ship). A technique costs the turn. "Modo Clássico" has no techniques and no luck.
- Coin toss of a Berry decides who fires first. The result is derived from the shared match seed, so both clients agree. Sea and weather (Mar Calmo, Grand Line, Novo Mundo, Tempestade) are visual by default. In Tático they may add a seeded effect, such as fog, that is identical on both clients.
- All randomness that affects the outcome comes from a seeded RNG (mulberry32) shared per match. Never use Math.random for rules.
- Every shot resolves to Água!, Acertou! or Afundou o <navio>!, then victory when the last ship is gone. A match produces a stats screen (shots, hits, accuracy, longest streak, turns, time, bounty).
- CPU opponents: Grumete (random), Marujo (hunt/target), Capitão (parity + target), Yonkou (probability-density over remaining ship lengths, with placement-bias learning across a series). CPU fleet placement is random, edge-aware or spread, per difficulty. Each rival crew has its own PT-BR taunts and reactions. A short "thinking" delay (400–1200 ms) keeps CPU turns readable.
- Controls: a crew selector, difficulty selector and rule toggles; hover highlight on the enemy plate; select a cell, then confirm with a FOGO! button (guards against mis-taps on phones); keyboard play with arrows + Enter and 1–0/A–J jump; a Log Pose hint button (limited uses, points toward the densest unexplored area); a "Diário de bordo" play-by-play feed; box score; scoreboard bug with turn timer. Everything is operable by keyboard and screen reader (aria-live for shot results).
- Cameras: duas águas (default iso view of both plates), Minhas águas, Mira inimiga (top-tilted aim view) and Panorama da Grand Line (whole scene, shows the sky island and the Marine fleet). Animation speed 1/2x to 4x (offline only), pause (offline only), skip ceremony, auto-next game, synthesised Web Audio (cannon boom, splash, hit crunch, sinking gurgle, a chiptune sea shanty loop, mute and volume), outlines/dither/raw 3D toggles, light and dark themes, prefers-reduced-motion support, mobile layout with drag-to-turn, pinch-to-zoom and a 2D grid fallback. Stats persist in localStorage, wrapped in try/catch.

ONLINE (PvP)
- Publish as an Artifact and use its shared-state runtime capability (load the host's artifact-capabilities guidance and declare exactly what it requires). Put all network code behind a Transport interface (publish, subscribe, read, heartbeat) so the same protocol runs on the host's store and on an in-memory fake for tests. If the capability is unavailable, disable PvP with a clear PT-BR message and keep PvC fully working.
- Lobby: "Criar sala" (5-character code and a shareable link), "Entrar com código", "Partida rápida" (open-rooms list with crew, board and rules), nickname (pseudonymous, no PII, random player id in localStorage) and preset-only reactions through the Den Den Mushi (e.g. "Boa mira!", "Prepare-se!", "GG, capitão!") with no free text, so there is nothing to moderate. Rematch button.
- The shared match record is an append-only, sequence-numbered move log; both clients derive the whole state by folding the log (so reconnecting is just a replay). Writes are idempotent (a duplicated or out-of-order event is ignored) and each turn is checked for ownership.
- Hidden information: the fleet NEVER leaves the owner's client. On "Pronto" each player publishes a commitment = SHA-256(fleet JSON + random salt) via WebCrypto. For each shot, the shooter logs the cell and the defender's client automatically answers with water, hit or sunk (sunk includes the ship's cells). On game end, both players reveal fleet + salt. Each client verifies the commitment and every logged answer against the revealed fleet; any mismatch shows "Trapaça detectada" and awards the match to the other player. Techniques are answered the same way and verified the same way.
- Liveness: a heartbeat every 3 s; "Adversário reconectando…" after 10 s; forfeit after 60 s; a turn timer enforced by both clients from log timestamps (a timeout auto-fires a random untried cell). Show connection state, round-trip latency and an "online agora" indicator. Handle a closed tab, a refresh mid-match (rejoin with a stored token) and being offline.
- Spectators (optional, read-only): they see shots and results but never any fleet until the reveal.
- Optional "Mais procurados" board of top bounties from the shared store, labelled as casual and unverified.
